# Working order: results → footing → narrative

**Where `data/<identifier>/` is**: `<tree>/docs/strategy-results-first/data/<identifier>/` — the material sits beside these documents, not at the project root. `<project root>/data/` is a different tree and will never hold it; a path written `data/<id>/…` below is that one.

**This file states the loop; the vocabulary lives one level down.** A reader holding only this file knows the shape of a round but not the nouns of the field it is run in: which quantity a row carries, what a "protocol" is called here, what the venue prints. That is by design — the loop is shared, the words belong to the instance (`src/template/instances/<id>/docs/strategy-roleplay/executor-notes.md`). Two consequences follow. An answer built on this file alone is correct in shape and silent in words; it must say so rather than supply a vocabulary. And when the words do exist in the instance, they are quoted, not paraphrased — a renamed quantity is a new quantity.

## 1 Core actions

Work in the order of the sections, one action each, and let each section's last sentence hand over to the next.

Every section carries three parts, written and read in this order: **action** says when it applies, what to do, and where to stop; **data contract** gives files and directories, fields and values, the moment of writing, and a countable check — missing one means not done; **criteria and breach** gives one runnable command or one number, and names the way of working that counts as a breach.

Every dispatch carries a `<wish>` taken from that entry's identifier in the wish list, and every state written afterwards lives under `tmp/compare-loop/<wish>/`. The body goes into the directory and filename the instruction names: no invented paths, no parallel tree.

`<round>` is the integer in `tmp/compare-loop/<wish>/round.json`, incremented when a round opens. An obstacle is worked around inside its own step rather than pushed to the next one, and "later" is never written into a file.

### 1.1 Thesis

The six blocks below are one chain, each eating the previous one's product: `drafting` writes the thesis, `placing hooks` nails every assertion to a registered paper, `stripping` removes what does not belong, `mapping numbers` makes thesis and report numbers line up, `rewriting the direction` turns a direction into decidable questions, and `handing downstream` passes the `qid`s to paving, running and drafting. A hole in any block stops the next one; nothing walks on with a gap.

The six blocks below are one chain, each eating the previous one's product: `drafting` writes the thesis, `placing hooks` nails every assertion to a registered paper, `stripping` removes what does not belong, `mapping numbers` makes thesis and report numbers line up, `rewriting the direction` turns a direction into decidable questions, and `handing downstream` passes the `qid`s to paving, running and drafting. A hole in any block stops the next one; nothing walks on with a gap.

**Thesis · drafting.** The thesis condenses what the final report presents: what, on what grounds, and where it stops — never how the work was done. One assertion per paragraph with its ground after it, ending at the Conclusion with no references section, bibliography or citation block. Section numbers are left to the mapping block; a body carrying "this chapter introduces" or "the following sections explain" is still report scaffolding and goes back.

**Thesis · placing hooks.** Go through the thesis sentence by sentence: each assertion carries `[key]`, and the key needs its `references.bib` entry and its `10_corpus/` package first, or it is `dangling` and is removed or registered in the same round. Hooks are the only form — entries, years, venues and pages live in `references.bib` and the corpus register, and 1.9 retells the hook, not the entry. What this block hands over is a thesis where every sentence has its ground; 1.7 and `1.9, Checking · reading sources` check against that same key list, and a key absent from it may not appear elsewhere.

**Thesis · stripping.** Empty the thesis of two kinds of text: work state (todos, rounds, script paths, line numbers) and accessories (abstract, keywords, figure list, acknowledgements, contents, repeated chapter titles). Afterwards each section opens with its claim, so first sentences alone carry the argument. This block removes characters, never judgements, and a place that will not come out means the judgement is unsettled and goes back a block.

**Thesis · mapping numbers.** Thesis numbers follow the argument, report numbers the prose, and equal digits are coincidence, so every pair is registered: `% SECTION <report section> <- thesis §<thesis section>` at the head of each report section and `→ report §<report section>` at the tail of each thesis section, one side shifted per round against the map. In the same block, align: every report paragraph points back at a thesis section and every thesis judgement lands in the report; what cannot land moves out or gets written. The two markers are where 1.9 writes back, and what the chapter-2 sections mean by "feed the thesis section" is these two lines.

**Thesis · rewriting the direction.** A direction — "bring the volume down", "run a comparison" — has no truth value, so its verb phrase becomes a yes/no or comparative question, one direction splitting into independent questions with dependencies recorded in `note`. Each lands in `questions.json` with `question` asking one thing, `answered_if` and `refuted_if` as observable conditions, at least two exclusive `arms`, `provenance` from `user`/`prior`/`literature`/`ours`, `section` naming the answering section or `dropped` with a reason, `pain_quote` as key plus sentence or `open` with "to be confirmed", and `needs_new` as `yes`/`no` with an experiment row or a stated reason. A changed direction rewrites the row in place, the old one recording `status: dropped` and "direction changed", with `invalidations.json` listing the invalidated `pid`s, experiment rows and paragraphs — rewritten, never appended, so downstream has one set of numbers.

**Thesis · handing downstream.** `qid` is the downstream `claim_id`: 1.3 keeps slots by it, 1.4 builds rows by it, 1.8 points each section's claim at it, 1.6's `footing.json` and 1.9's cross-section check use it, and nothing renumbers. One command is the criterion: print the `questions.json` rows missing a field, carrying fewer than two `arms`, carrying an empty `answered_if` or `refuted_if`, or marked `needs_new: yes` with no experiment row; an empty output enters 1.3, and each printed row is fixed by the three exits above.

### 1.2 Comparison

This section walks from a set of samples to a column of `next`s, one job per block, each handing the next its input: `build the ruler` picks the set and registers each sample, `counting each sample` measures every sample-section into a row, `building the range` folds rows into per-section bounds, `writing the table` turns bounds into what each paragraph of this draft owes, `measuring the draft` compares `target` and `current` row by row, and `judging` produces `pass`/`fail` with a `next`. Change the set and the first three rerun while the last three rejudge every section, not only the new ones.

**Comparison · build the ruler.** The ruler is a set of comparable finished texts, never one text, because position comes from the distribution. Same register and page band into `samples/`, each registered with its register and page count; when only one can be found, say why a set is impossible rather than using one text as a ruler. Sample metadata has two layers — `chapters[]` governs structure and a parser generates it, `writing.*` governs how a section opens and lands its judgement and has to be read — and the criterion comes from whichever shape passes in `report/samples/README.md`. It hands over the set plus one registration row per sample; the next block measures exactly that list.

**Comparison · counting each sample.** One row per sample per section in `samples/<sample>.position.json` with `gives`, `words`, `sentences`, `paras`, `cites_per_1000`, `basis`, where `gives` names what the paragraph delivers (conclusion, comparison, mechanism, boundary) and never "reads well". Read the sentences, not a summary; a paragraph whose function cannot be named records `gives: unclear` and the sample is swapped. It hands over rows per sample-section, which the next block stacks into a range.

**Comparison · building the range.** Stack the previous block's rows per section into `samples/<set>.range.json` with `min`, `max`, `median` and `n`; a section with `n` of 1 is reference only, and a section with fewer than three writes in `note` why the set is short. Once a range exists every section of this draft has bounds, and the next block turns bounds into "what this paragraph owes, how many words, how many citations".

**Comparison · writing the table.** The range gives bounds; the table gives this draft's specific debt. `position/table.json` holds one row per paragraph with `section`, `para`, `gives`, `words_target`, `cites_per_1000_target`, `source`, `order`, and `source` points at a row of the range file rather than at a sample's wording. Paragraphs are ordered by `gives` — conclusion, comparison, mechanism, boundary — and that order is where 1.3 takes its section sequence and what 1.8 reads before writing; a paragraph absent from the table is not written, and adding one means adding a row first.

**Comparison · measuring the draft.** Per table row compute `target` and `current` over five kinds — volume, citations per thousand words, position coverage, literature gap, slot gap — adding rows in the same shape to this round's `rows` when more kinds are needed. `target` comes from the range's `min` and `max`, never from one sample, with the path in `target_source`; `current` is measured the same way every time: volume from compiled pages, words and floats; citation density from body word count; coverage from the table against `sections.json`; the literature gap from `paper` rows in `stack.json`; the slot gap from `open` rows in `placeholders.json`. It hands over `rows`, and the next block reads nothing else.

**Comparison · judging.** `verdict` is `pass` or `fail`, each failing row carrying a `next` (`section`, `action`, `target`) whose `action` is one verb and one object — an action that cannot be written means the row was not measured properly and goes back. A row unchanged for two rounds records `fail`, writes `escalate: user` and pushes a `stack.json` row with `dedupe_key` and `priority`, at least one dequeued per round. The `next`s are exactly what 1.8 rewrites and 1.7 thickens, so by the time this section closes the next round's work list already exists on disk.

**Comparison · recording judgement.** Beyond the numbers, each section gets one `judgement` row placing it in the set — below the floor, inside the range, above the ceiling — with a page or paragraph; judgement places, it never praises. A changed set is recomputed whole: the range reruns, `basis` records which set and which range, the change goes into `stack.json`, and every finished section is rejudged. A baseline is measured the moment the ruler exists — a starting point, not a passing grade — and measured again at each round's close with the same method, so the difference is what the round actually did.

**Comparison · judging breach.** The range file exists with four non-empty fields; one row per sample-section with no missing field; every section has its range row or the round fails; one table row per paragraph with non-empty `source`, body paragraphs equal table rows; a range living only inside `verdict.target` fails the round; a section with `n` of 1 used as a criterion voids it; a `target` taken from this draft is void. Each of the seven is a command, run before delivery; a `pass` in `rows` whose criterion does not print zero is recorded as `fail`.

### 1.3 Paving

Paving assumes the result is coming and places it first, handing over in eight blocks: `writing the function sequence` says which claim a section makes, which number redeems it and which paragraph it lands in; `ordering sections` turns that into one `plan.json` row (claim, `qid`, what it follows); `scouting supply` finds supply or names a `gap` for every number; `building the skeleton` lays sections and one opening sentence in the table's order; `keeping slots and marking state` turns supply into `placeholders.json` rows and `% PENDING` lines; `recording obstacles` holds what was tried and failed; `reserving a section` holds the one whole section three sources agree is missing; and `judging the round` decides whether 1.4 can start. The whole draft is paved, finished chapters included, and a hole in any block leaves 1.4 without a clean input list.

**Paving · writing the function sequence.** One line per paragraph: which claim comes first, which number redeems it, which paragraph it lands in, which paragraph follows. The sequence holds function names only — conclusion, comparison, cost, boundary, hand-over — never sentences; a position with no statable function means the section has no claim yet, and "write something and see" is not a substitute. It hands over function lines, and the next block orders them.

**Paving · ordering sections.** Order the previous block's lines using the range file and the question table: each section states its claim, the `qid` answering it, and what it follows, written into `plan.json`; a section with no statable claim records `no-claim` and says why it stays. The order is not free — it comes from the `order` column of 1.2's table and matches the sample's sequence — and these rows are 1.8's work order, so sections are added or removed here rather than during drafting. It hands over one row per section, and the next block finds a number for each.

**Paving · scouting supply.** Every row answers four questions: is the data on disk, does the entry run, is the paper on the library, is the command reproducible. What is found becomes `supply` (path and command), what is not becomes `gap` (what to build, by whom); a `supply` is a command that pastes and runs, and "we have the data" is not one. Scouting judges presence, not difficulty — a hard row still records its `gap`, and difficulty is 1.4's ordering problem. It hands over `supply` or `gap` per row, and the next block turns them into slots.

**Paving · building the skeleton.** Write sections and order from the `section` sequence in `samples/<sample>.position.json`, adding nothing that file lacks, and give each section one sentence of body — that sentence is the section's claim. The skeleton lands as headings and opening sentences in the body; `out/` and configs wait for 2.5. A skeleton that is not laid leaves 1.8 with nowhere to write. It hands over a compiling skeleton, and the next block keeps slots in it.

**Paving · keeping slots.** One `placeholders.json` row per number, with `% PENDING <pid>` alone on its line in the body; an unimplemented entry raises `NotImplementedError("<pid>: <waits_for>")` and exits non-zero printing the `pid`, and an entry that writes an empty result file is not acceptable. `state` is `open`, `filled` or `dropped`, a filled slot recording its paragraph hash; every open slot needs `supply` or `gap`, and no row may have an empty `owner`. It hands over 1.4's input list: one open row, one experiment row — or one `blocked` note.

**Paving · recording obstacles.** A paper with no repository, an unobtainable full text, short compute, a run that still fails: write the obstacle and the routes tried in `note`, push one regurgitation row as `1.5`'s conflicting-number rule says, and keep the slot `open`. The note names which three commands were tried and which line they died on, because "it does not run" lets 1.4 repeat the same dead end. It hands over open rows with their obstacles, and the next block asks whether the gaps are structural.

**Paving · reserving a section.** Compare the section's `gives`, the position table and the sample's sequence; when all three point at one whole missing thesis section, record `action: reserve` in `plan.json` with its place, its `gives` and its supporting body section, and the same row in `idea-feed.json`. The reserve is not written now — 1.9 inserts it — and two of three sources agreeing is not enough, low confidence reserves nothing: a wrong section costs more than a missing one. It hands over the reserve rows 1.9 inserts and 1.8 knows about.

**Paving · judging the round.** Every section has a `plan.json` row with `claim_id` or `no-claim`; a dependency cycle or an unowned section leaves the round unfinished; a reserve row missing one of its three items does not count; and every open row carrying an `owner` ends as an experiment row or a `blocked` note before the section is handed over. One command judges it: print the `plan.json` rows that are missing, the `no-claim` rows without a reason, the open rows without an `owner` and the reserve rows missing an item; an empty output enters 1.4.

### 1.4 Running

This section turns a number from wanted into reproducible, handing over in seven blocks: `taking the target` states which sentence the number serves, under which four settings, and at what magnitude it counts; `checking four things` establishes that it can run at all; `building the list` writes one `experiments.json` row and orders it; `running and fixing in place` gets a number out of a pasteable entry and lands it in `intake.json`; `recording commands and pinning the baseline` keeps a trace anyone can replay; `judging done` decides whether it may be marked `done`; and `collecting decisions, listing refusals` holds what would not run and what may not be done. If the first block cannot be written, 1.3's slot is still empty and the round goes back rather than running while guessing.

**Running · taking the target.** One number, one experiment, and three things written first: which sentence uses it, which four settings it assumes, and how large a value counts. If those three cannot be written the slot is still empty — go back to 1.3 and fill `waits_for` and `owner`, then return. The target lives in the row's `expects`, never only in the conversation: it is both 1.5's comparison table and the only basis for judging whether the number is good enough.

**Running · checking four things.** Before the list: interpreter and dependencies, compute entry (local, cluster, scheduler), existing scripts and configs, and the convention for logs and result directories. Reuse what exists; where nothing exists, say what is missing and who fills it. Starting without these four means the first failure lands on the environment rather than the method. The findings go into each row's `needs`, so the next person prepares from the row instead of re-checking.

**Running · writing experiment rows.** One experiment per `experiments.json` row, ten fields: `id`, `serves` (`pid` or `claim_id`), `entry`, `needs`, `expects`, `state`, `number_key`, `fixes`, `degraded`. Run first whichever row is most likely to produce the target in one pass; order the rest by dependency. A long dependency is never moved back for being hard, only for being unready. The list doubles as 1.5's intake table: `serves` names the slot, and intake admits by the same slot.

**Running · fixing in place.** Get the smallest command printing one number before scaling to the full run; `entry` pastes into an empty shell and does not lean on state left by the previous command. A number goes into `intake.json` the moment it exists and its slot turns `filled` — otherwise it does not exist on disk. Each fix of dependency, path, seed, memory or split appends one `fixes` line; three fixes without progress on the same spot changes route, recorded in `decision.md` with the new route; a new problem is pushed into `stack.json` and the next row starts rather than waiting.

**Running · recording commands.** One command per `runs.tsv` line, `attempt` from 1, never merged, reworded or tidied afterwards; every failure appends the first twenty lines of `stderr` and the next command to try — that line is the only evidence of the failure, and "it does not run" records nothing. Before the first run, repository and commit go into `baseline.sha` as two lines and stay read-only; the round opens a `budget.tsv` line (hours, commands) and closes it with actuals, and a round over budget by a third with the delivery incomplete says why in `decision.md`. What it hands over is a trace for every number, which 1.6 cites when it writes `footing`.

**Running · judging done.** Done means the same command twice with byte-identical outputs (`cmp` empty); one successful pass does not count, and a third failure on the same command changes route instead of a fourth attempt. One command judges completion: list the rows with an empty `state`, the values an order of magnitude off comparable work, the `done` rows with no `intake.json` row or an unfilled slot, and the `blocked` rows missing `tried` or `proposal`; an empty output hands over to 1.5, and an off-magnitude value is checked against the settings first, `suspect` and stacked when the cause cannot be found. Anything non-zero keeps this section out of 1.5.

**Running · collecting decisions.** What will not run is collected in `decision.md`, one item per line — symptom, three commands tried, who decides what — never scattered across messages or left in memory. Five refusals, each checkable by a command: claiming a run without a number (rows in `runs.tsv` against artefact rows), substituting a nearby number (`number_key` against `expects`), bending the criterion to fit (`baseline.sha` edited), quietly shrinking scale (`degraded` empty), and marking unfinished work `done` (no `intake` row).

### 1.5 Intake

This section turns artefacts on disk into numbers a body can carry, handing over in eight blocks: `opening each item` reads what is inside, `writing records` puts one `intake.json` row per item, `checking settings` points the four settings at the sentence that will use them, `verifying by eye` lands doubts on page and cell, then `flagging suspicion`, `routing to running` and `recording conflict` each take their own branch, and `checking six fields` with `judging the round` closes it. It hands over two indexes, `intake.json` and `flags.json`: 1.6 admits numbers by those line numbers and `1.9, Checking · reading sources` traces back through the same ones, so a number absent from the index may not appear in the body.

**Intake · opening items.** Open every item and look at the contents: cells in a table, labels in a figure, the last lines of a log. Registering from a file name or directory name counts as not done. Note only what the item contains; whether it holds up is the branches' business. Each item gets crossed off the pending list once opened, and an item never opened does not enter `intake.json`, nor is "roughly like this" a substitute for opening it.

**Intake · writing intake rows.** One `intake.json` row per item, saying which number it produces, which version or run it came from, and the four settings it is read under (`dataset`, `split`, `metric`, `budget`). The row is the anchor for everything after: 1.6 admits by it, 1.9 traces by it, 1.2 counts the literature gap by it; a row that cannot name its origin means the item is not in yet.

**Intake · checking settings.** Each of the four points at the body sentence that will use it and writes the quantity: `dataset` to a section and sentence, `split` to a ratio or split name, `metric` to the measure's wording, `budget` to its unit. All four pointing gives `indexed`; one failing gives `incomplete`; unreadable content gives `unreadable`. The three states are three different debts — `incomplete` sends the body back for a sentence, `unreadable` sends the item back to 1.4 for logs or a rerun, and only `indexed` lets 1.6 admit the number.

**Intake · verifying by eye.** Land each check on a place: which page the number sits on, what the cell at row and column reads, how far two numbers differ; whether the measure named in the heading is the same word as the column; what the sample size is written as and which magnitude the conclusion uses; whether page and line numbers run without gaps. The result is one `flags.json` row with `where` naming page and cell and `evidence` naming the two numbers — a check with no page and cell counts as unchecked.

**Intake · flagging suspicion.** Fabrication, omission, weakness and doubt record `state: suspect` plus a `flags.json` row whose `kind` is `fabrication`, `omission`, `weak` or `doubt`, with `where`, `evidence` and `needed`. Suspect material does not enter the body as a conclusion; if it must be mentioned, the body writes `% PENDING <pid>` with "to be confirmed", and without that row it is not mentioned. This branch differs from `verifying by eye` only in consequence: verification records a place, suspicion keeps it out of the body.A missing result written up as a result, or a nearby number standing in for the target, is `kind: fabrication` outright — not an omission.

**Intake · routing to running.** A target number missing from disk or too weak to use goes back to 1.4 as an experiment row, with the intake row's `needed_by` naming that `id`; walking on without the row leaves the gap to be discovered during drafting. Before turning, confirm in 1.3 that the number has a slot — no slot, no run on its behalf.

**Intake · recording conflict.** The number is registered as it stands, the same `qid` records `status: refuted`, and `regurgitation.json` gets `kind: conflict` with the data's location, the original claim verbatim and the affected paragraph numbers. The counter-example is never hidden and the claim is never reworded to fit a number not yet rechecked: the round either changes the claim (through 1.9) or rechecks the number (back to 1.4).

**Intake · checking six fields.** Every artefact in `MANIFEST.json` carries `path`, `sha`, `command`, `source_version`, `number_key` and `idea_line`; a row missing one is not intake. One command judges completion: list the items without a row, the rows with an empty `state`, the rows with an empty `setting` or `evidence`, the `suspect` rows without a `flags.json` counterpart, and the `unreadable` or `incomplete` items missing from the readings; an empty output hands over to 1.6. Registering without opening counts as not done, and "just rerun it" in place of checking settings counts as cutting corners unless a rerun was actually asked for. Anything non-zero keeps this section out of 1.6.

### 1.6 Admitting

This section puts numbers into the body, handing over in five blocks: `connecting a number` writes it into its slot and leaves a `footing.json` row, `rechecking one cell` recomputes it from that cell's command alone, `reconciling` fixes the narrative rather than the number with conflict taking its own exit, `reconciling three places` makes package, body and thesis agree word for word, and `coupling changes` updates the four affected places in one round. Miss one block and the number gets cited next round as "probably right".

**Admitting · connecting a number.** Prove the number holds before it enters the body. Put it into its `pid` in `placeholders.json` cell by cell and write one `footing.json` row: which file, which run, under which four settings, and which body sentence uses it. If any field cannot be written, the number has no right to the body yet — a number with no citable origin cannot be rechecked by anyone and stays permanently suspect.

**Admitting · rechecking a cell.** Run one separate recheck per admitted number: only that cell's command and input, and only a reproducible same number counts as holding. What cannot be recomputed goes back to 1.4, and "we ran the whole thing" is not a substitute. The recheck command and its output go into the round's readings, one command per number; a cell that fails twice changes route by 1.4's rule rather than repeating here.

**Admitting · reconciling a cell.** When slot and number disagree, the narrative changes — wording, position, or one cell split into two — never the number, and never the criterion bent to fit. A number off expectation is first checked against settings and measure, and an unexplained one records `suspect` and is stacked; a real conflict is admitted as it stands, the same `qid` records `refuted`, and the conflict stays on disk. Three exits, one owner each: narrative to the paragraph, measure back to 1.4, genuine conflict to 1.9 to change the claim — there is no fourth exit that smooths the number.

**Admitting · reconciling three places.** Intake and admission reconcile in the same round: artefacts under the package's `out/`, `% PENDING` lines in the body, and the thesis section that matches must agree on numbers and file names word for word. Disagreement is fixed on the spot, or the stubborn place is pushed into `stack.json` naming all three; two of three aligned means the third fails next round.

**Admitting · coupling changes.** Once a package's result is admitted, the same round changes the four affected places: the body paragraph, `packages.tsv`'s `slot`, the thesis section, and the `refs.tsv` citation row. Changing one leaves three for the next round, and each change leaves one readings line (file and line number) — an empty line counts as unchanged.

**Admitting · judging the round.** One command judges admission: list the numbers without a `footing.json` row, the cells without a recheck record, the rows where the three places disagree, the coupling places missing their readings line and the `suspect` or `refuted` rows without a home; an empty output lets 1.8 draft on that cell, and the fix belongs to the block that owns it, not to "next round".

### 1.7 Thickening

This section turns "this section is thin" into "several more citable papers and details", handing over in seven blocks: `naming the gap` points at the sample and says whether comparison, mechanism or boundary is missing, `checking the library` rules out what is held, `getting the text` lands the body, `converting to text` produces a searchable `full.md`, `reading the text` copies settings, comparisons and pages, `writing three places` lands the records, and `access and maintenance` make citations and entries agree. What cannot be obtained goes onto the stack, and counts close the round.

**Thickening · naming the gap.** Thickening adds literature and narrative detail in the same round: literature by the sample section's citation distribution (how many citations per paragraph), detail by how the sample's paragraph unfolds. Never by total count or line count; afterwards the gap must be statable against the sample — "what this paragraph lacks is comparison, mechanism or boundary" — or nothing was thickened in the right place and the row is reread from 1.2's table. The gap becomes one readings line: section, kind, how many citations, which paragraphs.

**Thickening · checking the library.** By `key`, title and DOI; a hit is used, never downloaded twice. A library without a hand-over contract is no basis for comparison: one paper, one package — `paper.pdf`, the `pdf-reader` peer package, `meta` — with the package name equal to the `references.bib` key; a package that will not come together means this citation is not made. Three lines record held, newly fetched and unobtainable, and only the newly fetched ones feed the next blocks.

**Thickening · getting the text.** Use an interface or a copy already on disk; fall back to publisher page, author page and institutional mirror one at a time, dropping a channel the moment it fails and recording every channel tried in `note`. What arrives lands in `<literature>/<key>/` as `paper.pdf` (or `paper.xml`); when all three fail the item goes onto the stack rather than coming back empty or leaving "not obtained" in the conversation.

**Thickening · converting to text.** Where the ecosystem has a parser, convert to structured text at `pdf-reader-flash/full.md` beside the body under the same key; where it has none, record a stack line naming the substitute and why. Page screenshots, hand copying and eyeballing the PDF count as not done — without `full.md` as the base, later page numbers and quoted sentences have nowhere to land.

**Thickening · reading the text.** Search within the parsed text, read the method and results sections, and copy the four `setting` items, the four `comparison` items and the page numbers; quoted sentences come verbatim from the body, never from the abstract and never paraphrased. Judge whether to cite: does it change a statement this section already makes? If not, record `cites: background` and say it is background only. Abstract-only items record `held-method-only` and supply no numbers; the block hands over a `meta` draft and the one or two sentences quoted.

**Thickening · writing three places.** In one round: `literature/meta/<key>.json`, `literature/notes/<key>` and one library index row, plus the citation entry in the reference library with key, authors, title, year, venue and DOI or URL. A batch arriving together is handled as a batch: deduplicate by key, DOI and title, admit one by one, then rebuild the index and check the count; a duplicate keeps one copy and records the other path in `note`. A difference of zero across the three counts is what lands this block.

**Thickening · maintaining access.** The body writes `\cite{key}` only and entries live in the reference library; every `refs.tsv` row appears in the body exactly once, zero or twice both being errors. An entry inconsistent with the four-part form is migrated in the same round to whatever it lacks, with one `migrated.tsv` line; migration asks only what is missing rather than working from a table of old styles. Access has one form, `<literature>/<key>/`, and the library is checked before a key is written.

**Thickening · judging.** An unobtainable full text, a missing setting, a missing reproduction entry or a new problem each writes one `stack.json` line and the next item starts — the stack is this section's only permitted pending list, and anything left in the conversation is lost. One command judges the round: list the keys whose three counts differ, the rows supplying numbers under a `status` other than `held`, the abstract-only items not marked `held-method-only`, the unobtainable items missing from the stack and the exhausted channels without a `requested` row; an empty output lands the batch, and exhausted channels are raised together in that same output. Open stack rows close only as `done` or `dropped`.

### 1.8 Drafting

This section follows the order drafting happens, twenty-one blocks each owning one stretch: the first six decide what a paragraph says and how, the middle three are the fixed moves per section and paragraph, the last eight make packages, library, figures, ports and ledger hold together. Checking is not a section at the end but four frequencies hung on the drafting itself: each paragraph checks its ground, each section runs three commands, each chapter checks that sample, thesis and code tree say one thing, and each round checks ports and ledger; the further the draft goes the finer the check, and a deviation is fixed where it appears instead of waiting for a close-out. Retelling may be thin while experiments run, since attention belongs to progress; come back to compare only when something is genuinely stuck and the expected intake or slots will not close. Items inside a block run in the order written and each block hands the next its product; a finished paragraph leaves a `sections.json` hash, a `voice.json` row and an `artifacts.tsv` row, and missing one means the paragraph is not written.

**Drafting · reading the sample.** For as many paragraphs as this section writes, read that many paragraphs of a sample sentence by sentence — the sentences, not the `gives` summary — and name what each does: conclusion, ground, cost, boundary, hand-over. A sentence whose function cannot be named was not read; one `voice.json` row per paragraph read, and every `pattern` copied must be findable in the original or it is void. A comparison table comes before drafting: two columns, counts from comparable texts and counts from this section, one measurement. Claiming a benchmark against a venue means opening three of its papers and reading titles, section order, first paragraph and first result sentence; a claim resting on regex counts only is zero occurrences.

**Drafting · seating claims.** Write a sample's paragraph functions line by line — conclusion, comparison, what others cannot do, boundary — as a table of functions, never as paragraphs to copy; then seat each claim in its cell, and a claim that will not seat is deleted or the section declares a new cell with its reason. Doing neither means there is no claim yet and 1.3 owes a `claim_id`.

**Drafting · opening the paragraph.** The first sentence states the paragraph's conclusion and names its object, the second gives the ground (a number, a citation, a comparison), the rest unfold cost and boundary; one paragraph, one point, splitting past the table row's `sentences`. Each paragraph is then read against the sample's sentence in that position — does it deliver a number, a condition, a boundary, a turn? — and a sentence delivering none is deleted rather than polished.

**Drafting · sourcing sentences.** A paragraph needing this draft's number that disk does not hold writes `% PENDING` with a `placeholders.json` row naming `waits_for` and `owner`; every finished paragraph carries its source — a claim, a key, or a `footing.json` row — and a new claim goes back to 1.1 first. When a reading or a reaction contradicts the section's claim, the section reopens instead of being patched sentence by sentence, naming the reading and the claim in `stack.json`.

**Drafting · keeping the voice.** Academic written prose only: declarative, third person, each sentence carrying its assertion and its ground. Four countable edges — one assertion per sentence; sentence length in words, section average at most forty-five with anything past sixty split; third person only; no scene-setting openings, questions or imperatives, and zero colloquialisms.

**Drafting · landing numbers.** A number without a source may not land: `path:line`, and for corpus statistics the file and the algorithm (regex, stretch, denominator). Every number has a replayable origin — `out/MANIFEST.json` with `run_id`, verbatim `command`, `seed`, `seconds`, `exit`, `log` — and what cannot be traced is deleted or rerun. Citation state is only the `% footing:` lines in `references.bib`; a benchmark claim carries title and `path:line`, either missing meaning unread; borrowed numbers are rechecked.

**Drafting · compiling one section.** Body text goes into the commissioned directory and file name, with no invented paths and no parallel tree; only the template's commands, environment and styles, a preamble change recording its reason in `stack.json` first, floats from the template's own environments. Compile after each section and drive errors and warnings to zero; then run the layout check and render pages (see `1.9, Checking · landing and layout`), because exit code zero proves pages exist, not that they are right. Read back twice — as author, can each sentence point at its claim, key or number; as reader, what must I guess — and guessed sentences get a stack row and an immediate fix. Measure the section against 1.2 for words and citations per thousand before the next. Rewrite whole paragraphs into the file, never by script, `sed` or string surgery.

**Drafting · reading seams.** Before any section, every chapter has its skeleton subsections filled in one round, zero chapters without one, and structure problems go back to 1.3 and 1.2. Read the neighbours first and state the seam in one sentence — this paragraph takes the previous section's last sentence and hands its own to the next; an unstatable seam is broken and the seam is what changes, not a transition sentence. Slots close at the chapter end: the last subsection is "Open items and where the readings come from", each item naming what is missing and which runnable entry fills it (`PENDING(fill: <entry>)`), with "to be added", "later" or "will be given" in place of `PENDING` counting zero. A chapter ends as the next chapter's first sentence with phrases like "closing in" and "handing to"; labelled `Paves` rows count zero.

**Drafting · retelling into the thesis.** A finished section returns to its thesis place located by content — heading, the section's `gives`, the first assertion — not by matching numbers; single-point problems are fixed on the spot with a rerun and a status line, systemic ones stop the round, go into `stack.json` and move to 1.9. Thickening an Introduction adds that section's related work (`refs.tsv` at least two rows), writes its promises as slots (`idea_line` and `ledger.tsv`), and places them in packages (`packages.tsv` `slot`) in that order. After thickening a section, paving a package or reordering a chapter, the same round writes the section number into `report/drafts`, the thesis `idea_line` and the three `handoff.json` lines; a missing one means the round does not count.

**Drafting · numbering.** A container holds subpackage directories only — zero loose files, zero non-subpackage directories, no `README.md`, `data/`, `common/`, `docs/`; numbering is `NN_name`, `NN.M_name`, `NN.M.K_name` with consecutive subnumbers, file numbers matching directories, gaps only for two reasons in `audit.tsv`'s `note`, and a chapter reorder changing the `<work area>/` prefixes in the same round. Source writes only into its own `out/`, holding artefacts rebuildable by one command plus `MANIFEST.json`; hand-edited artefacts go to `out/manual/` and are named in the readings, and `out/`'s top level holds only `results.json`, `MANIFEST.json`, `runs/<run>/seed<NN>.png`, `manual/<original>`. No `common/`, `core/`, `shared/` or `tools/` in a layered package — shared logic exists as a `<package>/src/kit/` copy whose `__init__.py` names its origin. Paving needs `src/run.py` exiting 0, a config in `conf/`, an artefact and `MANIFEST` in `out/`, and a `README` whose first paragraph states the four slots; empty packages and source-less ones each get an `audit.tsv` row.

**Drafting · filing into the library.** Adding starts with three lookups (key, title, DOI) and uses a hit; otherwise fetch the text — interface first, then publisher page, author page, mirror, dropping a failing channel at once — landing `<literature>/<key>/paper.pdf`, and what fails goes into `stack.json`'s `paper` row naming the channels tried. Reading runs `pdf-reader --flash` to `full.md` (stitch split reads, delete parts after an empty `diff`), copying the four `setting` items, four `comparison` items and the limitations page, sentences verbatim. An entry holds four things — body, `full.md`, `meta.json`, optional figures — with `meta`, `notes`, index row and library entry written in one round and the three counts equal. Keys have one shape, `<surname><year>`, lower-case alphanumerics, at most twenty characters, year last; a rename writes the old-new map and makes bib key, `\cite` and directory the same key. Access has one form, `<literature>/<key>/`, the library is checked before a key is written, three pieces must be present before a number leans on it, the body writes only `\cite{key}`, and every `refs.tsv` row appears in the body once. Maintenance deduplicates by DOI, package path and section number; migration is an action — fix what the entry lacks, one `migrated.tsv` row, no table of old shapes; a messy library stops work, and the library readings line opens every intake.Residue counts as untidied and is handled in the same round: empty directories and an empty `chapters/` are deleted, second-hand notes (`notes.md`, `README.md`, `summary.md`) are folded into `meta.json`'s `notes` and the original removed, and a name that yields no author and year (`j`, `j2`, a hash suffix) is keyed from `meta.title` or goes to `requested`; handing over "leave it for now" counts as not handed over.

**Drafting · placing slots.** Before creating one, write which thesis assertion needs it, which `\ref` waits for it, and which number will appear in `out/`; failing that, go back to 1.3. The first file is a four-paragraph `README` — thesis section, entry command, what `out/` holds, what counts as passing — and intake rechecks against it. Every package has an `anchor.tsv` row and every artefact finds its place there; open items are `needs_paper`, `needs_reading`, `needs_slot` or `parked`, the first three cleared before delivery. Seven things make paving: a body section, a `plan.json` row, a slot, a `README` pointing at it, an artefact and `MANIFEST` in `out/`, non-empty `chapter` and `slot`, a `ledger.tsv` row.

**Drafting · tidying.** Before any move or rename, write the old-new list into the readings and check against it; a move without a list is loss. Deletion prints the list with counts first and irreversible work is backed up. Classify each item into one `tidy.tsv` row: a subdirectory passing none of four questions — own entry, own config, own artefacts, a name already in the chapter order — folds into `conf/` or `_deprecated/`, and numbers go only to real subpackages. One package at a time, three readings immediately (top-level entries, entry exit code, artefacts in `out/`); a `sha256` listing and entry output before and after must differ by zero, else roll back and record `impact`. State lives in the name — `_upstream/`, `_deprecated/`, `_misc/`, `_tmp/` — junk names are deleted, and there is no bin outside those three.A directory with only a `README` and no sources, and a package that is nothing but empty directories, count as untidied: give it an entry or fold it into `_deprecated/`, never let it sit inside a number as finished work.

**Drafting · drawing figures.** Each figure writes the sentence it supports with its `path:line`; no sentence, no figure. Three files: vector PDF, 300 dpi PNG, separate caption, no caption inside the figure, first caption sentence the conclusion and second the reading with n. `<package>/figures.py` rebuilds all figures into `<package>/output/figures/` and `report/final/figures/`; hand-drawn figures do not count.

**Drafting · writing ports.** A port is one hand-over: who gives what to whom. Each gives four values — trigger, artefact with path and fields, criterion as a command or a number, failure path — and a missing one fails, with "handed over" counting as nothing. Each port lands one line in the readings, every path existing on disk, a line pointing at a missing file void for the round; a criterion reading "checked" or "fine" counts as unjudged, and every failure path names a step this document contains.

**Drafting · writing the hand-off.** Every round closes by writing the hand-off into `handoff.json`; the four lines are defined in one place only — `1.9, Retelling · writing the hand-off` — one file per round, never appended to, never rewriting the last round's; a section-level rewrite copies the original to `/tmp/<project>_b<round>.md` and names the path, and a rewrite without a path is unrecoverable. Two rounds failing one criterion stop the third at "which command, which line" with a stack row; case artefacts under `tmp/reproduce-test/<id>/out/`, loop artefacts under `tmp/compare-loop/<wish>/`, zero `loop/` at the root. Before delivery ask three questions: absent from disk, obtainable this round, who decides otherwise. A returned reproduction walks five steps that day: read the export, fix the case, place the needle, judge verdict and fed, write back to the clause. Each assignment of a day gets one `chains.tsv` row with `order`, link sequence, break point and missing contract. Chains hand over five ways: thickening gives `idea_line` and `ledger.tsv`, paving gives `slot` and the entry command, experiments give `MANIFEST` and `footing`, order changes give the section map, closing gives `handoff.json`.

**Drafting · running and fixing.** Every run leaves one replayable record: the verbatim command on a `runs.tsv` row, the first twenty `stderr` lines and the changed line on failure, a second run exiting 0 with an empty `cmp`, `out/MANIFEST.json` with its six fields, `budget.tsv` with plan and actual, and `baseline.sha` with repository and commit — missing one keeps the round's numbers out of the body. An event is handled in the round it appears, not the next one: a landed `paper.pdf` is read into `full.md` and `meta`; a finished `full.md` has `chapters/` stitched to an empty `diff` before parts are deleted; a rename changes the bib key so `keydiff` agrees three ways; a new `\cite` finds its package and a `refs.tsv` row; a paved package gets its four-paragraph `README` and `mechanism.md`; an admitted number gets `footing.json` and a recheck; a chapter reorder changes five numbering places; three failures change route into `decision.md`; a returned case is read and fed the same day; a closing round writes four `handoff.json` lines. The files each own their place: budget `budget.tsv`, delta `impact.tsv`, fake-checking `fake.tsv`, the ruler `baseline.sha`, narrative `seams.tsv`; a missing file is the round's unpaid link. Any file created or rewritten adds an `artifacts.tsv` row in the same round — path, `sha`, command, section served — and without it the artefact counts as absent; drafting takes its slot row from `ledger.tsv` first and goes back to 1.3 when there is none; any experiment runs `python3 src/run.py --smoke` first, a non-zero exit forbidding the run, with the exit code and command into `runs.tsv` verbatim.


**Drafting · checking each paragraph.** A paragraph is checked as it lands: Every sentence needs one of three: a claim, a citation key, or a `footing.json` line number; with none, the sentence is deleted or turned into a slot. Every question in `questions.json` needs both `answered_if` and `refuted_if` or it leaves the question table; every slot needs `waits_for` and `owner` or it leaves the body; every key must exist in the library index. Cross-references to missing figures, tables and sections are fixed until they resolve. The block hands over a sentence list: each kept sentence with its ground, each deleted one with a reason. Every key in the body has a library entry carrying authors, title, year, venue and DOI or URL; every citation carries a `chapters/` or `full.md` `path:line`, and a missing line number counts the same as a missing file. Cross-section claims are compared by `claim_id` on numbers, units and qualifiers only, and any difference is reconciled or one side deleted. This block judges whether the ground can be opened, not whether it is right — that belongs to the next block. Each paragraph leaves two lines — which line every number points at and whether that ground opens; what fails is deleted or turned into a slot on the spot, never carried to the section's end.

**Drafting · checking each section.** A section is checked as it closes: A check never quotes its own last conclusion: every number's source is retraced to a `path:line`, every new key is looked up in the key table again, and the comparison table's two columns are recomputed under one measurement; the results go into `audit/<round>.json`, and any that moved since the last round says why. The feed is compared against the body before delivery, `grounds` is a file with a line number or a statistical method, and "as above" or "see earlier" is treated as no ground at all. Every line of `<work area>/<literature>` has a same-key entry in `references.bib` and vice versa, with both difference sets empty from one command each. Key shape gives three readings: directories not ending in a four-digit year, longer than twenty characters, or with an empty `meta.year` (unless listed as undated and uncited). Content gives two: `notes.md`, `README.md` and `summary.md` count zero, and `meta.json`'s `notes` is an array. Uniqueness gives two: duplicate DOIs and duplicate normalised titles both zero. Entries missing `full.md` count zero, and every `blocked.tsv` row carries `stderr` and a suggestion. A numbered directory keeps no leftover shells or temporary trees: outside `find <package> -type d \( -name '_deprecated' -o -name '_misc' -o -name '_tmp' \)` there is no bin, and everything inside names its destination; junk file names count zero; `tidy.tsv` rows equal the number of leaf packages, with no empty `state`. Three commands, and a non-empty output sends the section back before the next one starts.

**Drafting · checking each chapter.** A chapter is checked as it closes on shape and place: All three routes leave a trace this round — comparison leaves a range and a position table, thickening leaves `meta` and an index row, running leaves `intake` and `footing` — and a missing route means half a round. Chains hand over five ways and one missing break snaps the chain: thickening gives `idea_line` and `ledger.tsv`, paving gives `slot` and the entry command, experiments give `MANIFEST` and `footing`, direction hands the section map to paving, and closing gives `handoff.json`. The block hands over a trace table that points each missing route at the section owing it. First whether the prose reads like something handable, counts only corroborating; the impression lands on a place — what this paragraph's third sentence gives, how the sample's sentence in that position differs — and an unstatable impression counts as unchecked. Where no difference can be named, 1.8 rereads the sample; wording is not fixed here. Files outside the commissioned directory count zero; every preamble change has its `stack.json` row; hierarchy inconsistent with the template counts zero. Figures, tables and scripts each carry one rebuild entry stating version or commit and one command independent of upstream layout, and assets missing an entry or a version count zero. After compiling, measure two things on the produced PDF and then look at rendered pages. Overflows and overlaps print one line per compile from `python3 2.body.py` — `[layout] text block … N problems`; page-by-page reading renders PNG through `--shots <dir>`, in page order, with every page carrying a table, figure or formula inspected and one `elements` line and one `defect` line per page. A table that will not fit wraps long content first, then adjusts column spacing, and only then scales; the text block comes from `styles.py`'s `text_block` rather than a second measurement. This layer checks agreement of measure — prose against the sample, against the thesis and against the code tree, and any disagreement sends that section back.

**Drafting · checking each round.** The round closes on ports and the audit: Every port gives four values — trigger, artefact, criterion, failure path — with the criterion a command or a number, and "checked" counting for nothing; each round ends with four non-empty `handoff.json` lines, and a section-level rewrite keeps `/tmp/<project>_b<round>.md` with that path in the readings. Finally `audit/<round>.json` records `round`, `unsourced`, `questions_missing_criteria`, `placeholders_missing_owner`, `cites_without_key` and `layout`, `layout/<round>.json` holds one row per page, and every exit taken is registered with its destination: 1.8, 1.7, 1.4 or 1.5, or `stack.json`.

### 1.9 Retelling

This section retells the round's conclusions into the thesis, eight blocks handing over in order: `checking what lags` confirms that retelling is where the next round starts, `fixing the scope` finds which thesis sections this round touched, `writing the sections` seats assertions, costs and boundaries in the thesis's own order, `keeping the form` and `two coordinates` strip work-state and make both coordinates name one number, `recording readings` and `writing four lines` leave the successor what it needs, and `judging the round` closes on six numbers. Skip the feeding and the next round re-commits the old thesis.

**Retelling · checking what lags.** The body is delivered and has passed 1.8's four checks, while the thesis — the design document `<design document>.md` with its Chinese introduction — is behind it. A thesis behind the body is what the next round reads as current, so retelling is the next round's starting point rather than a closing chore; the block confirms one thing only: a round that changed the body owes a feeding, and skipping it is recorded as `unmet`.

**Retelling · fixing the scope.** For every section this round changed, find its thesis counterpart; a section with no counterpart gets a `stack.json` line and a new thesis section written in the same round, rather than having its judgement hung under an unrelated heading. The scope is enumerated section by section as "body section N → thesis section M" lines, one per changed section; one line short means one section has no landing.

**Retelling · writing the sections.** Write the round's assertions, costs and boundaries into those sections, seated in the thesis's own structure (question, claim, evidence, boundary, chapter by chapter) rather than copied in body order. What enters is judgement, not process: one assertion, its ground, and where it stops — the body's transitions, repetitions and work notes stay out.

**Retelling · keeping the form.** `待办`, `TODO`, `本轮`, `已完成` and `下一步` never enter the thesis; the document and its snapshot change in the same round with equal section and entry counts. The other half of the form is citation: hooks are `〔key〕` after the sentence and entries live in the library, with zero `dangling` — every key in the thesis resolves.

**Retelling · writing two coordinates.** `body_file:body_line` names the body sentence and `idea_line` names the thesis line, both pointing at the same number; one coordinate alone counts as not fed. Both go on one `idea-feed.json` row that also carries a non-empty `idea_section` — a number has a coordinate on each side, and losing one makes it impossible to tell next round whether it is still the same number.

**Retelling · counting sections.** Three lists go into `verdict`'s `readings`: sections changed this round, sections matched in the thesis, sections written anew. Where they differ, the difference has a `stack.json` row naming the section that did not match and why.

**Retelling · writing the hand-off.** The round ends with four `handoff.json` lines — conclusion, residue, never again, `next_entry` — so the next round starts there instead of rereading everything. One sentence each: the conclusion names the judgement this round established, the residue names what has not landed, and never again names the line this round crossed. A fourth line, `next_entry`, says where the next round starts — one command, one file, one criterion; four lines with this one empty counts as not handed over.

**Retelling · judging the round.** One command closes it: list the changed sections without an `idea-feed.json` row or with an empty `idea_section`, the work-state words left in the thesis, the document-and-snapshot pairs where only one side changed, the delivered sections with no retelling, the `dangling` hooks, and the missing map rows or references to sections that do not exist; an empty output counts as handed over.

## 2 Small loops

The road of chapter 1 has to be walked through these situations, and two of them are the main loops running side by side: 2.3 builds the sentences, 2.4 thickens them, 2.5 puts numbers on disk, and the two alternate; the other sections are the preparation before them and the finish after them. Each section is one stretch, not a list of steps: what you hold, what you look at first, which steps you walk (every one of them belongs to chapter 1), and what the stretch looks like when it is done or where it sends you back. The criteria are not restated here; they sit in the step each section names, and a situation that keeps recurring folds into the same paragraph rather than growing a new line.

### 2.1 Tidying the library

A library is rarely short of papers; it carries the same paper under several names and in several bodies. This stretch of the road makes the library into what `1.7` and `1.8` both recognise: beside a paper sit only its body, `full.md`, `meta.json` and an optional figure directory, the name comes from the key shape in `1.8`'s drafting, and there is one body only. Before touching anything, look at what is actually on disk — which directory names are not keys, which entries have no body, which still carry a second-hand note; that look is not a count, it is knowing how many you are about to move. Then move them one at a time: renaming a directory changes the library key to the same characters, a missing body is read again on the spot, a duplicate keeps one entry under its DOI or title with the other's notes merged across, and the extra note file is deleted with its content folded into `meta.json`. The criterion is not restated here: it is the two checks in `1.8`, and an empty output from the name check and the body-shape check means this stretch is done.

It looks right when any entry you open has its key as the directory name, its body present, its notes inside `meta.json`, and a same-key entry in the library. It looks wrong when every entry needs a sentence explaining why it is special, or when a paragraph of prose has to assure the reader that the batch is fine.

Any of these means the stretch is not done and you do not move on: an empty directory or an empty `chapters/` still sitting there; a name that yields nothing (`j`, `j2`) or carries a hash suffix; a package name that does not match the paper title, or a key whose last four characters are not a year; a second-hand note (`notes.md`, `README.md`, `summary.md`) still beside the body; a directory renamed while the library key and the body's `\cite` stayed as they were. Every one of them is visible to a single `ls`; leaving them in place writes "nothing wrong" into your own readings.

Say the whole thing plainly: the lazy part of library work is never the hard part, it is the part one glance would fix — empty directories, names like `j2`, keys renamed halfway. Leave those, and `1.7` cannot resolve a key, `1.8` cannot land a `path:line`, `1.9` cannot reconcile three places, and the whole chain is wasted.

### 2.2 Tidying a code tree

A root usually holds three kinds of thing: packages worth keeping, residue that never got cleaned, and directories nobody can name. Stand at the root and look down: classify each directory as container, leaf package, external clone or residue, on four facts — its own entry, its own configuration, artefacts somebody cites, a name the chapter order already reserved. A directory with none of the four is residue: fold it into the configuration directory or `_deprecated/`, and numbers go only to real leaf packages. Seeing `_upstream/` or `.git` means a clone; do not take it apart.

Before moving anything, write the old name and the new one; move one package at a time, with `git mv` only and not one new file; after each package take three readings — top-level entries, entry exit code, `out/` checksums — and write them back on that package's row. Keep a checksum listing before and after, roll back on any difference, and record what downstream it touched. Then walk `1.8`'s check for the section, so tidying does not break the paths the draft already uses.

It looks right when any numbered directory you open holds only real subpackages, its entry runs with one command, and everything in `out/` can be rebuilt from sources. It looks wrong when the tree looks clean but nobody dares touch it, or a numbered directory still carries shells named `1111`, `misc` or `v1_patched`.

### 2.3 Narration

Narration is the loop that runs beside the experiment loop: the experiment puts numbers on disk, narration puts sentences in the draft, and they alternate; neither stands in for the other. This section builds the sentences — the thesis, the places of the sections, each section's claims and boundaries, and the literature that serves them.

When the sentences will not stand, go back to `1.1` and write the claim and its boundary plainly, then to `1.3` and give it a place: a slot, the numbers it will need, the chapter skeleton — one missing and the place is filled first. With a place in hand, draft per `1.8`: one claim per sentence, every sentence naming its ground — a claim, a literature key, or a slot. Making a standing sentence thicker is the next section's job: thickening thickens narration only, and does not replace sentences that already stand.

Where narration has moved, tidy the layer under it in the same pass: check the new citations against `2.1` for the four pieces and the key shape, and look through the packages it touched per `2.2` for copies and residue that grew back. Top and bottom must read the same — the key the thesis names, the package the draft names, the directory on disk: a mismatch is one thing to examine. Old debt is fixed by supplying the missing piece; an unrun result written as run is flagged per `1.5` and stacked. Leaving both in place is not allowed.

When narration grows enough to change the thesis, feed it back once, in measure: the new claims and boundaries return per `1.9`, both sides naming one number. Not every paragraph needs that return, but nothing may be said only in the draft. Where narration exposes a number it does not have, it leaves the slot and sends it to `2.5`; an old number never props a new claim, and no conclusion changes inside the narration.

It stands when the section can say, pointing at the draft, what it establishes, which sentences carry it, and where it still waits for numbers — and a reader finishes knowing what the work does and what is missing. It does not stand when words outgrow the material: citations added without a sentence changed, whole paragraphs held up by "to be added", or the thesis and the draft saying different things. Closing, the promise returns to the thesis and the package: one `idea_line`, one `ledger.tsv` row, the package seated in `packages.tsv`'s `slot`.

### 2.4 Thickening

Thickening thickens narration: a section that has to say more says it through literature, other people's practice and what that practice costs, not by writing longer sentences. It usually happens alongside the tidying that serves it — new citations enter the shelf per `2.1`, numbers it needs run through `2.5` — but thickening itself moves sentences only, never conclusions.

Before starting, say which kind of thickness is missing: evidence sends you to `1.7` for literature and practice, a comparison leaves a slot for `2.5`, a boundary that is too loose gets written down. Every addition states three things: the claim it adds, the existing sentence it supports or replaces, and the page; missing one and the addition leaves the body. Citing without saying what changed, recording "read" as "added", filing from an abstract alone — none of that is thickening.

Thickening never changes a conclusion on the way past and never props a new claim with an old number: where it exposes a number it does not have, it leaves the slot and sends it to `2.5`. It looks done when the section is one layer thicker — a new claim, more citations, each pointing back to the sentence it changed; it looks unfinished when nobody can say which sentence moved, which is literature added without narration thickened. Closing, the promise returns to the thesis and the package: one `idea_line`, one `ledger.tsv` row, the package seated in `packages.tsv`'s `slot`, and the new citations walked once through `2.1`.

### 2.5 Experiment

The pieces exist: assertions in the thesis, slots in the draft, packages with shapes and entries, and nothing but the numbers missing. This section runs the numbers and brings them back, with one feed before the run and one after — the expectation written down first, the result connected afterwards. A retest, an extra comparison, a robustness arm, an equivalent refactor: all of them are the same act here, and what moves is the number, never the conclusion.

Feed before the run. Per `1.3`, turn the question being asked into a slot: which claim this experiment answers, what its criterion is, what a failing result would look like; then per `1.4` write the experiment row and put the expectation into the slot. Without an expectation, a number that comes back can only be talked into fitting.

Run per `1.4`: get the smallest sample to run and print one number, then widen to the full set; write the command verbatim, keep `MANIFEST` with its six fields, record the budget and what it actually took; three failures on the same error in one place means writing down the line, what was tried, who decides, and changing the route instead of grinding. Runs that will not move are raised in one place, not scattered through messages.

Bring it back per `1.5` and admit it per `1.6`: open every item and look at its content, register the four settings, verify by eye, flag what looks wrong; give every number its `footing` (which file, which run, under which four settings, which sentence uses it), recheck one cell, reconcile package, draft and thesis to the character, and change the four coupled places in the same round. A number that has not reached the draft means the round did not run.

As the run comes back, tidy the package it came from per `2.2`: `out/` keeps only what sources rebuild, anything edited by hand is stored aside and named in the readings, copies are deleted and residue folded away, and `README` and `MANIFEST` must agree. This is not extra work; it is why the next round can run at all. When a number and the top layer disagree, decide first whether it is old debt or fabrication, then change the number or flag it.

As the round closes, turn what is still queued into something that can be handed over: completed rows carry three pieces of evidence (the row says done, the intake table has it, the slot is filled), rows stuck three times on the same failure are blocked with what was tried, what is missing and who decides, the rest keep a priority. When a number is questioned or the environment moved, retest: same command, same input, same seed, two artefacts byte-identical — equal logs and equal runtimes are not passing. A new comparison, a robustness arm, another scale: that is a new experiment row, a new number, never an old number edited into the shape you wanted. Before changing a structure store the baseline, and after the change the two artefacts must still be byte-identical — the ruler (criteria, tests, baseline) does not move by a single character. A package that runs but cannot say whom it serves is pinned back: `chapter` and `slot` on its row, the `idea` coordinate in `anchor.tsv`, and the command chain from entry to artefact, all three saying one thing.

It works when every number traces to one run, every row has an owner (the finished one points at a number, the blocked one at a command, the rest at a priority), the `README` handed to somebody else yields the same number, a retest produces two identical artefacts, and a refactor produces exactly what it did before. It does not work when a single trial run is counted as intake, a blocked row is written as done, the criteria are edited until the checksums agree, or the package is complete and nobody can say which sentence it serves.

As it is handed over, feed back: one weakness line (which package, which criterion failed, how the next round prevents it), one `next_entry` (where the next round starts), then retell per `1.9`; without that line the same task is not re-issued.

### 2.6 Packaging

Packaging is finishing to the point the user asked for, not finishing the project. The deadline arrived, or a presentable draft is wanted now; this section decides how far the round goes and what it looks like. Start from what you hold: numbers back and figures drawable, or only skeletons and slots — that decides the route.

With numbers in and figures drawing, package the normal way: connect the numbers through `1.6`, draft them into sentences per `1.8`, measure each section per `1.2`, and load section by section up to the point the user named. This route is about accuracy: every number has its `footing`, every sentence points at its number and its slot.

When the material is short, packaging is not an unconditional hand-over of half a draft but a comparison first: set this draft beside comparable drafts and read what still keeps it from looking finished. Whatever is missing is made up from narration — `1.7` for literature and other people's practice, `1.3` for places and slots, `1.8` to make the sentences stand; when a number has to come from an experiment, judge the cost first and run only what closes inside one round (a basic finish, the few numbers narration needs, then stop), never opening an expensive loop at this hour.

Figures and numbers are best; without them, hold up what the comparison and the pass judgement look at: a layout that stays in bounds, one register throughout, a conclusion in each section's first sentence, positions matching the sample. No blank figures as placeholders, and no unrun number written as a conclusion.

Before handing over, sweep once through `2.1` and `2.2` and tidy end to end: at the top, whether the thesis and the draft sections still match this round's numbering and wording; at the bottom, whether the library and the code packages carry copies, residue or hand-edited artefacts from this round. Every mismatch becomes one line — old debt names the missing piece, fabrication is flagged per `1.5` — and nothing goes out on "it roughly agrees".

Before handing over, walk `1.8`'s four checks (and say which group was not walked), retell per `1.9`, write four hand-off lines — conclusion, residue, never again, where the next round starts — plus one weakness line and `next_entry`. Prepare the answers the user may ask for at any moment: where things stand, what this section says, what each number is and where it came from, and what the next step is. Four questions unanswered means packaging is not done.
